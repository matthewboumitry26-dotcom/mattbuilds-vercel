How the next generation can use AI in the family business

Most family businesses haven't started with AI yet. That's an opening for the next generation, if you bring it in the right way.

If you work in a family business, you've probably heard "that's how we've always done it." Bringing in new tools can feel like stepping on toes, especially when the people who built the company still run it.

AI is one of the easiest places to start, and one of the biggest gaps. In PwC's Global NextGen Survey 2024, 73% of next-generation family business members said generative AI is a powerful force for transformation. Yet only 7% of family businesses had implemented it anywhere in the business, and almost half (49%) had either banned it or not started exploring it.

Canadian businesses are catching up quickly. Statistics Canada found that 12.2% of businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services in the year to mid-2025, double the 6.1% a year earlier. Here's where I'd start.

1. Start with the paperwork nobody likes

Drafting emails, summarising meeting notes, cleaning up quotes and writing job postings. These save time without changing how the real work gets done, so nobody feels replaced.

2. Write down what's in people's heads

A lot of what makes a family business work lives in the founder's head. Record a conversation about how a job gets planned, then use AI to turn the transcript into a checklist the whole team can follow.

3. Use it to analyse, not to decide

AI can help compare supplier prices in a spreadsheet or spot patterns in past jobs. The decision, and the responsibility for it, stays with you.

4. Protect what isn't yours to share

Don't paste client names, pricing or drawings into public AI tools. PwC found only 6% of family businesses had rules for using AI responsibly, so agree on a simple one before anyone starts.

5. Win one small battle first

Don't pitch AI as a new way to run the company. Pick one task, save a few hours, and show the result. A small win earns more trust than a big presentation.
